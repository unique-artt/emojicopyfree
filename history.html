The Complete History of Emojis

From 176 hand-drawn icons in 1999 to over 3,000 global symbols used 10 billion times a day — this is the full story of how emojis conquered the world.

📅 Updated October 2026 ⏱️ 8 min read 🏷️ Emoji History

Every day, humans send over 10 billion emojis across WhatsApp, Instagram, iMessage, and email. They feel timeless — like they've always existed. But emojis are younger than Google, younger than the iPhone, and younger than Wi-Fi. They were born from a very specific problem: text messages were too short, and misunderstandings happened too easily.

The word "emoji" comes from Japanese: e (絵, "picture") + moji (文字, "character"). Literally: "picture character." This is the story of how they came to be.

🕰️ The Emoji Timeline

💡
1997

A Problem on a Tiny Screen

Shigetaka Kurita, a developer at Japanese telecom giant NTT DoCoMo, was working on the "i-mode" mobile internet platform. The screens were tiny — just 12×12 pixels — and text messages were capped at short character counts. Kurita saw a problem: short text leads to misunderstandings. His solution: tiny pictures that carry meaning.

💭 Fun fact: Kurita wasn't a graphic designer. He studied economics, and he drew the first emojis by hand — with pencil and paper.

✏️
1999

The First 176 Emojis Are Born

Kurita released the first emoji set: 176 images (some sources say 180) created for NTT DoCoMo's i-mode. These weren't just smileys. They included:

  • 😊 Facial expressions — for tone and nuance
  • ☀️ Weather symbols — inspired by Japanese forecasts
  • 🚗 Transportation icons — trains, cars, planes
  • 💡 Objects and numbers — practical mobile tools

Many were inspired by Japanese manga, weather symbols, and kanji. The whole set was black and white, but it changed how Japan communicated.

📱
2009–2010

Emojis Go Global: Enter Unicode

For a decade, emojis stayed in Japan. But a problem emerged: emojis sent from one Japanese carrier wouldn't display on another. They needed a universal standard.

In 2009–2010, the Unicode Consortium — the organization that standardizes characters across all computers and phones — adopted emojis into the Unicode Standard. This was the single most important moment in emoji history.

🌍 Why it mattered: Before Unicode, an emoji sent from a DoCoMo phone wouldn't display on a SoftBank phone. Unicode made emojis universal.

🍎
2011

Apple Makes Emojis Mainstream

Apple added a native emoji keyboard to iOS 5 in 2011. Combined with the explosive growth of the iPhone, this took emojis from a Japanese phenomenon to a global obsession. Google followed with Android soon after.

By 2012, emojis were appearing in text messages, tweets, and Facebook posts worldwide.

🎨
2015

Skin Tone Modifiers Arrive

For years, every human emoji was yellow. In 2015, Unicode added five skin tone modifiers, based on the dermatology Fitzpatrick scale. Every human gesture — 👍 🙏 💪 — could now show different skin tones.

This turned a limited set into thousands of combinations and was a major step toward representation for non-white users.

⚧️
2016

Gender-Neutral and Inclusive Options

Unicode introduced more inclusive emojis — gender-neutral people, same-sex couples, and professional roles for all genders. Emojis were becoming a mirror of the world, not just a set of cartoons.

🧕
2019

Hijab, Mate, and Period Emojis

Unicode approved emojis representing the hijab, the mate drink, and — after years of campaigning — a period emoji 🩸. These additions signaled that emojis were no longer just for fun. They were a form of global language and identity.

🏛️
2016

The Original Emojis Enter MoMA

The Museum of Modern Art (MoMA) in New York acquired the original 176 emoji set by Shigetaka Kurita as part of its permanent collection. Emojis had officially become art history.

🌐
Today (2026)

3,000+ Emojis, 10 Billion Sent Every Day

The Unicode Standard now includes over 3,000 emojis — and thousands more combinations when you count skin tones and gender variants. Emojis aren't just for texting anymore. They're used in:

  • 🛒 E-commerce — shoppers search products by emoji "vibe"
  • ⚖️ Law — courts decode emoji meanings in messages
  • 📊 Data — over 470 million smileys sent on Twitter daily
  • 🎨 Art — MoMA collections and emoji-based design

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Who invented emojis?

Shigetaka Kurita, a developer at Japanese telecom NTT DoCoMo, created the first emoji set in 1999. He drew 176 original icons by hand.

When were emojis invented?

The first emoji set was released in 1999 in Japan. Emojis went global in 2009–2010 when the Unicode Consortium adopted them into the Unicode Standard.

What does "emoji" mean?

"Emoji" is Japanese: e (picture) + moji (character) — literally "picture character."

How many emojis exist today?

Over 3,000 emojis are officially recognized by Unicode, with thousands more combinations when you count skin tones and gender variants.
